The story

You might be where I was 13 years ago.

My son stopped wanting to live, not because of who he was, but because of how I responded to who he was.

He is 23. He is a transgender man. He is an artist in Bilbao. He illustrated this book. He made the cover sculpture. He is the reason any of this exists.

The journey between the father who was certain and the father who understood took thirteen years. This book is the map I did not have.

A father's map

23 years from uncertainty to unconditional love

2002-2013

The performer

Valentin played the role he was given. Ricardo believed it completely. A child learns early which version of themselves is safe to show. A father learns late what he was actually looking at.

The signs were there from the beginning. The summer Valentin stopped wanting to wear dresses. The art teacher who recognised something the school administration saw as threat. Ricardo watched all of it and filed it away under phases.

2013

The first call

Valentin calls. Non-binary. Ricardo has no map, no framework, no language for what he is being told. His first response, delivered with love and total certainty: this is a phase.

He researched. He came back with gentle but corrosive logic. The conversation Ricardo thought was open-minded was, in fact, the first brick in a wall he did not yet know he was building.

2013-2014

The wall

A father who would die for his child, doing everything wrong. Certainty as armor. The distance grows without either of them naming it. The damage compounds quietly, brick by brick.

Ricardo built elaborate theories. The mother-rejection theory. The cultural-contamination theory. Both made his certainty the solution and his son's experience the problem to be solved.

2014

The checkmate

Valentin calls again. He feels like a man. He has felt this way since he was four or five. Ricardo's answer: antidepressants, continue therapy, push through it.

Valentin complied, because he was a good son. Dad, I hate this. I feel like a zombie. I cannot paint. Ricardo insisted he stay on the medication. That is a sentence Ricardo still carries.

2014-2015

The wake-up call

Valentin calls a third time. He has lost the will to live. Ricardo is 39, in Barcelona. This is the call Ricardo will spend the rest of his life being grateful he answered differently.

Something broke open. A father ran out of moves and found, on the other side of running out, the only thing that had ever been available: presence without an agenda.

2015-2023

The long repair

Shadow work. Self-forgiveness. Valentin's forgiveness. The slow, unglamorous work of becoming someone his son and his younger children could actually reach.

No single moment. No dramatic turn. Years of reading, meditation, therapy, courses. The relationship rebuilt itself the way bones do after a fracture, stronger at the break.

2024

The sculpture

Valentin gives Ricardo a clay sculpture for his fiftieth birthday. Inscribed with a sentence that took twenty-three years to earn: gracias por evolucionar conmigo.

He called it 10 Lustros. He made it himself, fired it himself, carried it himself. It sits on Ricardo's desk now.

2025-2026

The book

Valentin is 23. A working artist in Bilbao. He illustrated this book and made the cover sculpture. He is thriving.

This book is not about Valentin. It is about Ricardo, about the mechanisms inside a loving father that nearly made him the cause of the thing he feared most. It exists now.
